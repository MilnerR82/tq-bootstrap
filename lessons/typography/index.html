Need a definition? ↓

Typography is how you arrange and style written language. Font choice, size, weight and space help people find the important parts and follow your message. You don’t need to draw letters or know any code to begin.

A little space goes a long way

Good typography helps a reader concentrate on the message. When lines sit too close together, it can be harder to follow one line and find the next. Space gives each line a little breathing room.

Long lines ask your eyes to travel further. Very short lines interrupt the flow. Try a few settings, compare the results, and choose a balance that works for your audience. There is no single setting that suits everyone.

Try this starting point: text size of at least 18 px, line spacing of at least 1.5×, a maximum width between 45 and 65 ch, and left alignment.

These are practice targets for this exercise, not universal rules or an accessibility pass/fail test.

Keep these close

A tiny type dictionary

Open a term for a quick reminder.

A typeface is a letter design, such as Georgia. A font is a particular implementation or style, such as Georgia Bold. A family groups related styles. In everyday use, people often use “font” for all of these.

Weight is how thick the strokes look: regular, medium or bold, for example. Use emphasis selectively. If every word competes for attention, the important words can be harder to spot.

Leading describes spacing between lines; on the web, line-height controls the line box. Tracking adjusts spacing across a run of letters. Kerning adjusts the space between particular pairs of letters, such as A and V.

Hierarchy shows the order of importance. White space is the empty space around and between content, whatever colour the background happens to be. Both can help readers group ideas and find their way.

Your takeaway

Clarity comes before decoration.

Choose a suitable typeface. Make the order clear. Give the text room to breathe. Then check it with the people who will read it.
